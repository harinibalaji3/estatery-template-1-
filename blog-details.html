Five Questions to Ask During Every Property Site Visit

By Ananya Krishnan September 28, 2026 6 min read
← Back to Blog

Photos and floor plans can help you shortlist a property, but a site visit gives you the opportunity to understand what living in the property will actually feel like.

Don't leave a site visit with only photographs. Leave with answers to the questions that matter for everyday living.

1. What will I actually pay every month?

Don't focus only on the purchase price or monthly rent. Ask about maintenance charges, parking fees, utility costs, association charges and other recurring expenses.

Understanding these costs before making a decision helps you create a more realistic monthly budget.

2. How reliable are the water and power supplies?

Check the water pressure, power backup and common facilities during your visit. Ask the property manager or residents whether interruptions are common.

3. What parking space is actually included?

Parking can become an important everyday issue. Confirm whether the property includes a dedicated parking space and where it is located.

4. Which documents and approvals are available?

Ask the seller or property representative about the relevant property documents and approvals. If you are seriously considering the property, have the documents reviewed by an appropriate professional.

5. What is the neighborhood like?

A property can feel very different at different times of the day. Consider visiting again during another time to understand traffic, noise, lighting and nearby activity.

Site Visit Checklist

Before leaving the property, check these simple items:

  • Check walls, ceilings and windows.
  • Test water taps and basic electrical points.
  • Ask about maintenance charges.
  • Confirm parking arrangements.
  • Check nearby roads and public transport.
  • Take photos and notes for later comparison.

Final Thoughts

A good site visit is more than simply looking at the rooms. It is an opportunity to understand the property, building, neighborhood and ongoing costs before making a decision.
